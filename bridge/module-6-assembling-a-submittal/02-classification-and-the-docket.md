# Classification and the Docket

Some of what a design-basis package contains would help an attacker. Most of it would not. Deciding which is which, for every passage in fifteen instruments, is a practice rather than a judgment call — and a practice is what this unit is about.

> **Where the regime is set out.** What the protection categories are, who holds the authority, and why a public docket matters to the legitimacy of the whole process are covered in [Module 4, unit 02](../../core/module-4-security-licensing/02-safeguards-information.md). Per [Conventions](../../CONVENTIONS.md), that unit owns the regime and this one owns the practice. What follows assumes you have read it and takes up the question of what an author actually does.

## The mechanism is structural, not editorial

The instinct is to treat this as a marking exercise: write the documents, then go through with a highlighter deciding what to withhold. That approach fails in both directions. It is slow, it produces inconsistent results across a large set, and by the time it runs the site-specific detail is already distributed through documents that were meant to be public.

The mechanism this corpus uses instead is the **class-versus-derivative resolution split**, and its whole virtue is that it sorts material at the moment of authorship rather than at the moment of filing.

**A class-level instrument is site-neutral by construction.** It states general findings about a facility class: how the class behaves, what barriers it credits, what the analysis method is, where the gaps are. It contains no particular plant, so there is nothing in it that targets one. It can be written to be public without anyone deciding to make it public.

**A derivative is where the site-specific detail accumulates**, and it accumulates there because that is the only place it can arise — when a class-level method is applied to an actual location. Vulnerability assessment for a real site, hardening parameters, discrimination criteria for a specific airspace, the particulars of a correlated event at a named campus. Every derivative is a protection-review object by construction, before anyone has read a word of it.

So the resolution split *is* the classification-management device. It is not a filing convention that happens to have a security benefit.

## The material test, and applying it honestly

The test is whether a passage would **materially aid an attack on, or the defeat of, a specific facility.**

Methodology passes and stays public. Architecture passes. Conformance matrices pass. Stated gaps pass — and that one matters most, for reasons Module 4 gives. Actionable site-specific detail does not pass.

Stating the test takes a sentence. Applying it is harder, and the honest practice is to name in advance the places where general treatment shades toward actionable and read those passage by passage rather than by category. For this package there are three: the uncrewed-systems vulnerability profiles, where the threat basis edges toward a how; the physical-proximity and seizure analysis, for the same reason; and the campus cyber material, where a description of a shared architecture's weaknesses is also a description of where to push.

Naming those three in the plan is itself part of the practice. It tells a later reviewer where the author was paying attention, and it means the formal review knows where to look.

## Authoring to the split

Four habits make the eventual review a confirmation rather than a rewrite.

**Write the class instrument as though it will be public, because it will be.** Not as a document that will be redacted into publishability — those read badly and leak their own shape.

**Push site-specific detail into a derivative the moment it appears.** If a paragraph in a class instrument has started naming a particular site's parameters, it has become a derivative paragraph and belongs elsewhere.

**Keep the gap statements in the public half.** This is the one that takes discipline, because a gap is uncomfortable and a protected annex is right there. Module 4 explains why over-protection is a failure mode; this is where that becomes an authoring rule.

**Write the disposition down as you go.** A short record of what is public, what is designated, and which passages were flagged for the review points above — produced alongside the authoring rather than reconstructed afterwards.

## What this does not settle

The classification and docket-handling plan is explicit that it **is not itself a classification determination**, and recommends a formal review by the authorities that hold that power before anything is docketed.

That posture is correct and worth understanding rather than treating as a disclaimer. Classification authority is not something a corpus can assume. A document that behaved as though it had would be both wrong and useless — wrong because the authority sits elsewhere, useless because the determination it purported to make would not bind anyone.

What an author can do is everything above, which is most of the value. Do it and the formal review confirms. Skip it and the choice at filing is between a bad disclosure and an expensive re-authoring of a fifteen-document set. The open form is [LI-12](../../register/LI-12-public-docket-and-safeguards-split.md).

## What a reviewer should press on

Whether any declared residual, conditionality statement or admitted gap has migrated out of the public half — the single most important question here and the least likely to be asked.

Whether the class and derivative boundary was applied while authoring or retrofitted, which shows in whether class instruments read as site-neutral or as redacted.

Whether the review points are named, or whether the plan asserts that the test was applied without saying where it was hard.

And whether a formal review is scheduled before docketing rather than assumed to have happened.

## What to carry forward

The class-versus-derivative split is the mechanism, and it works because it sorts at authorship rather than at filing. The material test is whether a passage would materially aid an attack on a specific facility; methodology, architecture and admitted gaps stay public. Name the places where the test is hard rather than claiming it was easy. And an author's plan is not a classification determination — the authority sits elsewhere, and the plan's job is to make that review a confirmation.
