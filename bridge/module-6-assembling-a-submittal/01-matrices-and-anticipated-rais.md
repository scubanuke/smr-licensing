# Matrices and Anticipated RAIs

A package can be complete and still be hard to review. The difference is whether a reviewer can move freely between the regulation and the record in both directions, and whether the questions they are going to ask have already been answered somewhere they will look.

Two instruments do the first job and one does the second.

## Two directions, and why one is not enough

Module 2 covered how to write a conformance matrix — the four columns, the three ways a conformance statement fails, the rule about naming gaps. This unit is about the **pair**, because a conformance matrix on its own cannot make a particular kind of failure visible.

The **conformance matrix maps outward**: from the record to the regulation. Every applicable provision, where the record addresses it, how it conforms. Read it and you can tell whether the regulation has been answered.

The **traceability matrix maps inward**: from each locus in the governing instrument to the instrument that carries the requirement and the obligation it satisfies. Read it and you can tell whether the record's own commitments have carriers.

Now the failure each one misses. A package can be **complete against the regulation** while an internal requirement — something the governing instrument itself states must be done — sits in no instrument at all, because no external provision happened to ask for it. Only the inward matrix catches that. And a package can show **every internal requirement carried** while an applicable provision was never mapped, because nobody listed it. Only the outward matrix catches that.

Run both and an **orphan becomes visible**: a requirement with no carrier, or a carrier serving no requirement. That is what makes the pair a completeness ledger rather than two tables.

## Reading the ledger

In practice the inward matrix is four columns and reads down the structure of the governing instrument: the locus, the requirement stated there, the satisfying instrument, and the trace. Something like twenty-four rows for this facility class, grouped the way the instrument is grouped — facility class, source term and emergency planning, adversarial basis and passive safety, the dual design basis, regulatory context and open items.

The open-items group is the most informative part of the whole ledger, because it is where the record admits what it has not closed and says who closes it. The counter-uncrewed-systems gap traces to its reframing memo. The campus cyber gaps trace to their decomposition. The reduced-planning-zone argument traces to its standalone technical basis. The machine-learning admissibility question traces to its qualification methodology. The siting question traces to the geopolitical siting instrument. The grid-interface question traces to a paper that has not been written, and the ledger says so.

A reader who wants to know what state this package is really in should read that group first. It is the honest summary the executive summary is not allowed to be.

## Anticipated requests for additional information

The third instrument pre-answers the questions a reviewer will ask. For this package that is roughly twenty, grouped by review area — scope and pathway, source term and emergency planning, passive safety and multi-module adequacy, the military-action extension and the threat boundary, security, cyber, artificial-intelligence governance and formal verification, and the grid interface — each phrased the way a reviewer would phrase it, each with a response and references into the record.

Two things make this work or fail.

**It has to include the pointed ones.** A set that pre-answers the comfortable questions has told a reviewer exactly which questions the applicant did not want to raise, which is worse than not doing it at all. The hard ones for this package are the ones worth listing out loud: the reduced zone does not bound the military-action case and is not meant to; the mechanistic source term is carried by design-family reference rather than reproduced; the seventy-two-hour figure is a floor and not the design duration; why the Commission should review military action at all, when a licensee is not expected to defend against a military; whether machine learning sits on any credited path; and whether a cyber compromise defeats passive safety, which it does not, though it can blind the people watching.

**It has to phrase the question as a reviewer would.** An anticipated question written in the applicant's preferred framing is not the question that will arrive. The useful discipline is to write each one in the most awkward form it could reasonably take, then answer that.

## Where these instruments sit relative to each other

The matrices are part of the package. The anticipated questions are a candidate work product, which is to say a vehicle rather than a core component. That is the right split: the ledgers are structural and a reviewer needs them to navigate, while the pre-answered questions are a courtesy that compresses the cycle and could be omitted without leaving a hole.

Which is also why the anticipated set can be honest in a way the matrices cannot easily be. A matrix cell has to state a conformance position. A pre-answered question can say *this is genuinely open, here is our view, here is the vehicle* at whatever length the answer needs.

## What a reviewer should press on

Whether both matrices exist, because one alone cannot show an orphan and the absence of the inward one usually means nobody checked.

Whether the open-items group traces every admitted gap to a named closer, or whether some trail off.

Whether the anticipated questions include the ones that are actually hard — the fastest check being to look for the reduced-planning-zone question and see whether it is asked in its sharpest form or a softened one.

And whether the matrices and the record agree, which a reviewer tests by picking three rows and following them.

## What to carry forward

The conformance matrix maps outward to the regulation and the traceability matrix maps inward to the carriers; neither alone makes an orphan visible, and together they are a completeness ledger. The open-items group of the inward matrix is the most informative thing in a package. Anticipated questions compress the review cycle only if they include the pointed ones, phrased as a reviewer would phrase them rather than as the applicant would prefer.
