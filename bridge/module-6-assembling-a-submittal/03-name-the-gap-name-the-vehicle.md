# Name the Gap, Name the Vehicle

Everything on this site has been building toward a rule that takes one line to state and is difficult to follow:

**Where the record does not conform, say so, say what would close it, and say who can move it.**

That is the house rule. This unit argues for it, sets out what a well-formed gap statement contains, and explains why the register on this site is what the rule looks like when it outgrows a single submittal.

## The argument from the reviewer's position

Most compliance cultures treat an admitted non-conformance as a finding waiting to happen, and the instinct they produce — assert conformance wherever a defensible reading allows it — is exactly wrong here. The reason is not ethical. It is that the instinct misunderstands what the reviewer is doing.

A reviewer is not looking for reasons to refuse. They are trying to **reach a documented conclusion about every applicable provision**, one that will survive an independent committee and a hearing. That is the job, and anything that makes the job possible is welcome.

A manufactured conformance makes it impossible. The reviewer must now discover the gap themselves, which takes time. Having discovered it, they must decide what else was papered over, which takes more time and is not a question they can close by reading — so they ask, and the requests multiply. A single dishonest cell has cost the applicant the reviewer's trust in every other cell, and trust is what a matrix is for.

A gap stated with its closure vehicle does the opposite. It tells the reviewer the applicant knows. It tells them the applicant has thought about what would resolve it. And it converts an open item from a deficiency the staff must characterize into a tracked action with an owner — which is something a Safety Evaluation can record.

## What a well-formed gap statement contains

Four elements. Missing any one turns it back into an admission.

**The gap itself**, stated as a specific shortfall rather than an area of uncertainty. "The campus-scaled critical digital asset scoping method does not exist" is a gap. "Cyber security for multi-module campuses is an evolving area" is a mood.

**The provision it sits under**, so the reviewer knows which finding it bears on — or, where there genuinely is none, the statement that there is none, because an unhooked gap is a different and usually harder problem and saying so is information.

**The vehicle**, meaning the specific instrument or process that would close it: a guidance update, a regulatory guide revision, a rulemaking petition, a statutory extension, an applicant demonstration accepted on the record.

**The owner**, meaning who can actually move that vehicle. Commission, industry consensus process, interagency, applicant, Congress. This is the element most often left out and the one that does the most work, because a gap without an owner is an observation, and observations do not close.

The phrase that fails all four at once is *will be addressed*. It names no shortfall, no provision, no vehicle and no owner, and its function in a document is to look like a commitment while being none.

## Where the honesty actually costs something

It is worth admitting that this rule is not free, or the argument sounds like a claim that candour is always costless, which it is not.

A stated gap is **quotable**. It can appear in an intervenor's contention, in a press account, in a competitor's comparison. An applicant who names four gaps and a competitor who names none will, on a superficial reading, look like the less ready of the two.

The rule survives that for two reasons. The gaps are generally discoverable anyway by anyone who reads the record properly, so the choice is usually between disclosing and being found out. And a package that names its gaps and routes them is describing a filing posture, not confessing — the twelve work products exist precisely because the gaps were named, which is what a reader who understands the process sees.

That does not make it comfortable. It makes it correct, which is a different thing, and the corpus should say so rather than pretending the cost is zero.

## The rule generalized

Follow this discipline through a single submittal and you get honest matrices. Follow it across a corpus and you get something else: a standing list of what is unresolved, each entry traced to its locus, its provision, its vehicle and its owner.

That is the register on this site. It is not an addition to the course half — it is the course half's rule, applied to everything at once and given somewhere to live. The seven fields each entry carries are the four elements above plus a locus, a dated state of play, and what turns on the answer.

Which is why the two halves of this site could not have been built separately. A course that taught the framework without the register would have taught a method and hidden its output. A register without the course would be a list of complaints with no account of why each one matters. Together they are one argument: here is how the framework works, and here is precisely where it does not yet reach.

## What a reviewer should press on

Whether every stated gap carries all four elements, and in particular whether it names an owner.

Whether any *will be addressed* survives in the document.

And whether the gaps named are the ones a knowledgeable reader would have found anyway — because a package that names only the easy ones has done the reputational cost of honesty without buying the benefit.

## What to carry forward

Where the record does not conform, name the gap, the provision, the vehicle and the owner. The argument is from the reviewer's position: manufactured conformance forces them to find the gap themselves and then doubt everything else. A well-formed gap statement has four elements and *will be addressed* has none of them. The honesty has a real cost and is still correct. And the register is this rule generalized past a single filing — which is why the two halves of this site are one argument rather than two.
