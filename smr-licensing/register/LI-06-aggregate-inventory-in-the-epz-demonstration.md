# LI-06 — Aggregate Inventory in the EPZ Demonstration

A single small module holds far less radioactive material than a large reactor, which is most of why its emergency planning zone can be smaller. A campus of a dozen modules holds rather a lot. Whether the zone is sized on one module's inventory or on the campus total is not a detail — it is the difference between two quite different numbers, and the guidance does not unambiguously say which one governs.

**The issue** — Whether and how the emergency-planning-zone demonstration must run on aggregate multi-module inventory, under correlated defeat of shared barriers, rather than on per-module inventory.

**Where it surfaces** — DBA-MA-SMR-FC1 §3A and its class-level source-term reference in §3A.7; developed in candidate work product 3A §4.

**Regulatory hook** — 10 CFR 50.47 and Appendix E as modified by the 2023 small modular reactor emergency-preparedness rule; Regulatory Guide 1.183; and, for the shared-barrier question, the General Design Criterion on sharing of structures, systems and components among units — see [LI-08](LI-08-gdc-5-sharing-of-structures.md).

**Why it is open** — **Guidance is absent.** The performance-based rule states a dose criterion and leaves the inventory basis to the applicant's demonstration. That is workable when a site is one reactor and ambiguous when it is twelve sharing barriers whose defeat correlates.

**Closure vehicle and owner** — Either an explicit statement of the aggregate basis in emergency-preparedness guidance, or an applicant demonstration that adopts it and is accepted on the record, becoming the referable precedent. **Owner: the Commission** for guidance; **the applicant** for the demonstration. The corpus takes the aggregate position outright: a per-module source term does not carry to a campus, and the demonstration must use the aggregate inventory.

**State of play** — *As of September 2026.* Open. Candidate 3A argues the aggregate basis; no endorsed guidance states it. This entry is tightly coupled to [LI-05](LI-05-reduced-epz-conditionality.md) and to [LI-07](LI-07-assc-duration-on-aggregate-demand.md), which is the same aggregation question asked about coping duration instead of inventory. If the aggregate basis is settled once, all three move together.

## Reader's stake

*For the practitioner* — An applicant who demonstrates on per-module inventory has taken on the risk that the first serious request for additional information forces a redemonstration on aggregate, late, with the site layout already fixed. Demonstrating on aggregate from the start is more conservative, more defensible, and — because this is the first real campus review — likely to become the referable precedent whichever way it goes.

*For everyone else* — "Less radioactive material" is true of one small reactor. A site with twelve of them is a different quantity, and which number the safety calculation used is a fair and simple question to ask at a public meeting.
