# LI-04 — State-Actor Pre-Positioning in the Threat Basis

Every reactor security requirement is measured against a defined adversary — a described set of capabilities the plant must be able to withstand. That description is set by the Commission, deliberately, so that licensees are held to a common and achievable standard. It describes a serious adversary. It does not describe a state intelligence service quietly establishing access years in advance and waiting.

**The issue** — High-tier state-actor pre-positioning is not represented in the design-basis threat against which licensee protection is measured.

**Where it surfaces** — DBA-MA-SMR-FC1 §7.4, third of the three stated gaps, and the three-tier adversarial basis of §4A. Candidate work product 1B routes it separately from the other two, on purpose.

**Regulatory hook** — 10 CFR 73.1, the design-basis threat itself, and the cyber-security regulatory guide that calibrates to it.

**Why it is open** — **The threat basis is not calibrated.** The construct is sound and was drawn around a different adversary. This is the one of FC1's three cyber gaps that cannot be handled through industry consensus, because the threat basis is not industry's to set.

**Closure vehicle and owner** — A revision of the cyber-security regulatory guide if the calibration can be handled at guidance level, or an outline for a rulemaking petition if it reaches the 73.1 construct itself. **Owner: the Commission and its staff, exclusively.** The corpus recommends explicitly that this run on a separate and slower track from [LI-02](LI-02-campus-scaled-cda-identification.md) and [LI-03](LI-03-shared-digital-ic-guidance.md), so that two tractable industry items are not held hostage to the hardest one.

**State of play** — *As of September 2026.* Open, and the least likely of the three to move quickly. Candidate 1B states the routing and the sequencing argument. Note the boundary the corpus is careful about: raising pre-positioning within the threat basis is not the same as asking a licensee to defend against a military, which is the separate postulate handled in [LI-11](LI-11-the-warning-pathway-and-who-decides.md) and in Module 5.

## Reader's stake

*For the practitioner* — Where this lands determines whether a whole class of protection obligations is the licensee's or the government's. Pushing it into the design-basis threat transfers cost and duty to licensees across the fleet, which is why it moves slowly and why the sequencing recommendation matters: a submittal that couples its two tractable cyber asks to this one has effectively deferred all three.

*For everyone else* — Plants are required to withstand a specific, written-down kind of attacker. A nation-state that spends years preparing is not what that description was built for. Whether it should be is a genuine policy question with real costs on both sides, and it is one only the regulator can settle.
