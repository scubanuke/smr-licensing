# How to Read the Register

The course half of this site teaches a framework. The register holds the places where the framework does not yet reach — sixteen questions that a small modular reactor licensing action currently runs into and cannot cleanly answer.

Each entry is one question. Not a topic, not an area of concern: a question, with a locus in the record, a provision it lives under, and a named party who could move it.

## The seven fields

Every entry carries the same seven, in this order.

**The issue** states the question in a sentence. **Where it surfaces** gives its locus — the instrument and section that raises it — because a question with no locus in the corpus is not yet a register entry, it is a thought. **Regulatory hook** names the provision it lives under, and where there genuinely is none, the entry says *none, and that is the issue*, because an unhooked question is a different and usually harder problem than a hooked one.

**Why it is open** is the diagnosis, and it falls into one of four kinds. *Authority is missing* — somebody would have to be given a power they do not have. *Guidance is absent* — the power exists but nobody has said how to exercise it at this scale. *The threat basis is not calibrated* — the construct exists but was drawn around a different adversary. *The method has not been developed* — the question is technical and nobody has built the machinery to answer it. Naming the kind is what makes the next field obvious.

**Closure vehicle and owner** says what would actually close it and who can move it: the Commission and its staff, the industry through its consensus process, the interagency, or the applicant. This is the field that distinguishes a register from a list of complaints. An entry with no owner is an observation.

**State of play** is dated and expected to go stale. **Reader's stake** is two short paragraphs, one for the practitioner and one for everyone else, saying what turns on the answer.

## What the register deliberately does not do

**It states questions, not answers.** Where the design-basis corpus has taken a position, the entry says so and points at the instrument that argues it — and then stops. The argument lives in the instrument, which is signed, versioned, and citable. The register's whole value is that it can be trusted to report what is genuinely unsettled rather than to advocate, and an entry that quietly argued its own answer would destroy that.

**It does not rank.** There is no severity column. Which of these matters most depends entirely on who is asking, and the register would be making that judgment for the reader if it pretended otherwise.

**It is not a prediction.** Several of these will be closed by rulemaking, guidance, or industry work already in motion. The state-of-play field is where that shows up.
