# LI-02 — Campus-Scaled Critical Digital Asset Identification

Cyber-security rules for reactors work by first deciding which digital equipment matters. You identify the assets whose compromise could hurt safety, security, or emergency preparedness, and you protect those to a high standard. The method was built when a site meant one reactor, or two. A small modular campus may run a dozen modules sharing a single control building — and an asset that is important for one module and *critical for all twelve at once* does not sit comfortably anywhere in the existing scoping method.

**The issue** — Critical-digital-asset identification was developed for single-unit scoping and does not cleanly handle a shared asset whose compromise is simultaneously consequential across many modules.

**Where it surfaces** — DBA-MA-SMR-FC1 §7.4, the first of three gaps that section states; §9.5 folds all three into one undifferentiated ask. Candidate work product 1B decomposes them and routes this one.

**Regulatory hook** — 10 CFR 73.54, implemented through the endorsed industry scoping guidance and the corresponding regulatory guide.

**Why it is open** — **Guidance is absent.** The requirement in 73.54 is perfectly capable of covering a campus; what does not exist is the scoping method that tells an applicant and a reviewer how to apply it when the asset's consequence scales with module count.

**Closure vehicle and owner** — An update to the industry critical-digital-asset scoping guidance and its endorsing regulatory guide. **Owner: the Nuclear Energy Institute**, developing it, with the Commission endorsing. This is an industry-led item by design: the endorsement mechanism exists precisely so that method-level questions of this kind are worked in the consensus process and then accepted by reference, rather than resolved case by case in twelve separate reviews.

**State of play** — *As of September 2026.* Open. Candidate 1B is drafted at v0.1 and states the routing; the corpus has not taken this to the consensus process. Note that the corpus's own treatment is constrained: industry guidance is copyrighted, so the instruments characterize its scoping construct rather than reproducing it.

## Reader's stake

*For the practitioner* — Left unresolved, this becomes a request for additional information with no good answer, because the applicant is being asked to apply a method that does not address the configuration. Naming it as a guidance gap with an industry vehicle is a materially better posture than attempting a bespoke campus scoping argument that the staff then has to evaluate without a yardstick.

*For everyone else* — The rules for protecting reactor computers assume a plant is one or two reactors. When a site is a dozen small ones sharing equipment, "which computers are the critical ones" stops having an obvious answer. Nobody has yet written down how to decide, and until they do, each applicant would be improvising.
