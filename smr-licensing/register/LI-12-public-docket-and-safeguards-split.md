# LI-12 — Public Docket and the Safeguards Split

Reactor licensing is conducted in public. That is not decoration: the legitimacy of the whole process rests on an affected community being able to read what was claimed, what was found, and on what basis. But some of what a design-basis analysis contains would be genuinely useful to an attacker. Those two facts pull against each other, and where exactly the line falls has to be decided rather than assumed.

**The issue** — Which material belongs on the public docket and which must be protected, when the analysis most useful to an adversary overlaps with the analysis the affected public has the strongest claim to see.

**Where it surfaces** — Developed in candidate work product 2E; the public-record argument it rests on is in the United States regulatory case at Level 1.

**Regulatory hook** — 10 CFR 73.21 and 73.22 for Safeguards Information; the separate regime for classified national-security information, which reaches intelligence-derived threat characterizations.

**Why it is open** — **The method has not been developed** in a form that can be applied consistently. The categories exist and are well defined; what is missing is a repeatable way to sort a design-basis corpus into them without either over-protecting the argument into uselessness or under-protecting specifics.

**Closure vehicle and owner** — The corpus proposes that the **class-versus-derivative resolution split is itself the classification-management device**. Class-level instruments are site-neutral and state general findings — they inform design without targeting a plant — and stay public. Derivatives accumulate the site-specific detail (vulnerability assessment, hardening standoff parameters, counter-uncrewed-systems discrimination, aggregate-inventory event specifics) and are designated before docketing. The material test is whether a passage would materially aid an attack on, or the defeat of, a specific facility: methodology, architecture, stated gaps and conformance matrices public; actionable site-specific detail withheld.

**Owner: the classification and Safeguards authorities**, formally. Candidate 2E is explicit that it is not itself a classification determination and recommends a formal review before docketing. **The applicant** owns applying the split as it authors.

**State of play** — *As of September 2026.* Open as a formal matter; operating in practice. The disposition is default-public for all class documents and candidate work products, with named review points where general treatment shades toward actionable — the uncrewed-systems vulnerability profiles, the seizure analysis, and the campus cyber material. Every derivative is a protection-review object by construction.

## Reader's stake

*For the practitioner* — Deciding this late is expensive, because it means re-authoring. Deciding it structurally — writing class instruments to be public by construction and pushing site-specific detail into derivatives from the start — costs nothing and makes the eventual formal review a confirmation rather than a rewrite.

*For everyone else* — There is a real reason some of this is withheld, and there is also a real risk in withholding too much: if the uncomfortable parts of a safety case can be moved into a protected annex, the public record stops showing what the plant actually assumed. The position taken here is that the *method and the admitted gaps* stay public even when the site-specific numbers do not — which is the part worth holding anyone to.
