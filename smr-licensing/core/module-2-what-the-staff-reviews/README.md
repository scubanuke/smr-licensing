# Module 2 — What the Staff Actually Reviews

## Learning Objectives

After this module, a reader will be able to:

- Name the principal content of a reactor application and the General Design Criteria it must address.
- Trace a single requirement from the regulation through the application to the staff's written finding.
- Explain why the volume of requests for additional information, rather than the technical difficulty of the design, is usually the schedule risk.
- Build and read a conformance matrix, and say what an honest one looks like when the applicant does not conform.

## What this module does

The previous module chose a route. This one is about what happens on it. A reviewer opens an application and has to reach a written conclusion about each requirement that applies — not an impression, a documented finding capable of surviving an Advisory Committee's scrutiny and a hearing. Everything in the review machinery exists to get from the applicant's claim to that finding.

The practical lesson of the module is that an application is easier or harder to review largely in proportion to how well the applicant anticipated this. A design can be excellent and still generate two hundred requests for additional information because nobody mapped it to the provisions it satisfies.

## Units

**[Application Content and the General Design Criteria](00-application-content-and-the-gdc.md)** — what goes in, and the criteria of Part 50 Appendix A it has to speak to. The criteria that do the most work for a multi-module campus are the ones on sharing of structures, systems, and components among units, and on protection against natural phenomena and dynamic effects.

**[Requests for Additional Information, the Safety Evaluation, and ACRS](01-rais-the-se-and-acrs.md)** — the review cycle in practice. What an RAI is and why it is a schedule instrument as much as a technical one. The Safety Evaluation as the staff's written product. The Advisory Committee's independent look, and the hearing that follows.

**[The Conformance Matrix](02-the-conformance-matrix.md)** — the keystone unit. Provision, obligation, where addressed, conformance statement. The recurring pattern worth internalizing: for most General Design Criteria a design-basis extension conforms to the *principle* — defense in depth, redundancy, independence — while extending the *application envelope*, and saying exactly that is more useful to a reviewer than claiming unqualified conformance.
