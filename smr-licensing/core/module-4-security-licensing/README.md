# Module 4 — Security Licensing

## Learning Objectives

After this module, a reader will be able to:

- Explain what the design-basis threat is, what it bounds, and who sets it.
- Distinguish physical protection, cyber security, and information protection as three separate obligations under Part 73.
- State the counter-uncrewed-aircraft authority asymmetry precisely, and identify whether it is a regulatory or a statutory problem.
- Say what may appear on a public docket and what may not, and why the distinction is not merely administrative.

## What this module does

Running alongside the safety case is a second regulatory world with its own logic. Safety asks what happens when things fail; security asks what happens when somebody makes them fail. The two have different criteria of adequacy and, for most of their history, different reviewers.

The organizing idea of this module is the **design-basis threat** — a defined adversary, with defined capabilities, against which a licensee must demonstrate protection. Everything in Part 73 is calibrated to it. Understanding exactly what it bounds is what makes Module 5 comprehensible, because the military-action extension is entirely about what sits above that boundary.

## Units

**[The Design-Basis Threat and Physical Protection](00-the-dbt-and-physical-protection.md)** — the threat construct of 10 CFR 73.1 and the physical-protection programme of 73.55. Why the threat is set by the Commission rather than by the licensee, and what follows from that allocation. The counter-uncrewed-aircraft asymmetry: a licensee may detect but may not defeat, while a federal site may do both — a gap that lives in statute, not in the regulation the licensee reads.

**[Cyber Security under 10 CFR 73.54](01-cyber-security-under-73-54.md)** — the cyber-security programme, the identification of critical digital assets, and the industry guidance the Commission endorses to implement it. Why single-unit asset scoping does not straightforwardly handle an asset that is critical for several modules at once. *Note: the industry guidance discussed here is copyrighted and is characterized rather than excerpted; see [Conventions](../../CONVENTIONS.md).*

**[Safeguards Information](02-safeguards-information.md)** — the protection regime of 73.21 and 73.22, and what it does to a public-record process. The tension at the centre of this unit is real and unresolved: the legitimacy of reactor licensing rests on a public docket, and the details most useful to an adversary are exactly the details an affected public has the strongest claim to see.
