# Course Map — Licensing the Small Modular Reactor

**Version 1.2.** This is the syllabus for the complete site; the folders mirror it.

The through-line is the **record**: what an applicant has to put in front of the Nuclear Regulatory Commission, what the staff does with it, and where that exchange currently has no settled answer. Seven modules build the framework in teaching order. Sixteen register entries hold what is open. Every module ends by pointing at the entries it governs.

> **The instruments this site draws on.** The design-basis corpus behind these modules — the SMR facility-class design basis, its four inherited core instruments, the supporting instrument set, and the twelve submittal work products — is inventoried with citable identifiers in the [Instrument Library](resources/instrument-library.md). Start there if you want the documents rather than the argument.

## Core — the framework

### Module 0 — Orientation
What a licensing action actually is: an application, a docket, a staff review, a finding. Who is in the room — the applicant, the NRC staff, the Advisory Committee on Reactor Safeguards, the Commission itself, the Nuclear Energy Institute acting for industry, and intervenors with hearing rights. Why a small modular reactor is not simply a small large reactor, and why the difference is a licensing problem before it is an engineering one.

**The reader picks a design family here** — pool-immersed integral pressurized-water, isolation-condenser boiling-water, or dry passive-air — and it threads through everything after. This is the analogue of the course's *pick your sector*, and it is not cosmetic: the credited barrier set differs by family, so every consequence argument downstream resolves differently depending on the choice.
Closes with: *Open Issues.*

### Module 1 — The licensing pathways
Part 50's two-step construction permit and operating licence; Part 52's design certification, early site permit, and combined licence; Part 53, the technology-inclusive framework finalized in March 2026; and Part 57, proposed in May 2026 for microreactors and comparable risk profiles. How an applicant chooses among them and what each choice commits them to. The ADVANCE Act sitting behind all of it.

Then the topical-report economy — the mechanism that makes any of this financeable. A topical report reviewed once and found acceptable becomes referable by later applicants, which converts a sunk regulatory cost into an asset. Understanding why an applicant writes to topical-report standard explains most of the form the corpus takes.
**Physically split:** the durable pathway material is separate from `90-snapshot-2026-rule-status.md`, which holds the current rulemaking state and is expected to age fastest on the site.
Closes with: *Open Issues.*

### Module 2 — What the staff actually reviews
The content of an application and the General Design Criteria of Part 50 Appendix A that it must address. The review machinery: the request for additional information and why its volume is the real schedule risk, the Safety Evaluation the staff writes, the Advisory Committee's independent look, and the hearing.

The keystone unit is the **conformance matrix** — the requirement-by-requirement mapping from a provision to the place the application addresses it to the statement of how it conforms. A Safety Evaluation is built on that spine, and an applicant who does not supply it has left the staff to construct it.
Closes with: *Open Issues.*

### Module 3 — Consequence and the source term
The chain everything downstream hangs from: source term to dose to zone. What a mechanistic source term is and why the certified quantities are carried by design-family reference rather than reproduced. The alternative source term of Regulatory Guide 1.183. The 2023 emergency-preparedness rule for small modular reactors and the performance-based, consequence-scaled planning zone it permits, sized so projected dose stays under the regulation's criterion of 1 rem total effective dose equivalent over 96 hours.

Then the catch that makes the whole module worth reading carefully: a per-module source term does not carry to a multi-module campus. The demonstration has to run on aggregate inventory.
Closes with: *Open Issues.*

### Module 4 — Security licensing
Part 73 as its own regulatory world running alongside the safety case: the design-basis threat of 73.1, physical protection under 73.55, cyber security under 73.54 and the industry guidance that implements it, and the Safeguards Information regime of 73.21 and 73.22 that decides what can appear on a public docket at all.

The unit that matters most here is the authority asymmetry around uncrewed aircraft — a licensee may detect but may not defeat, while a federal site may do both. That asymmetry is a statute problem wearing a regulation's clothes.
Closes with: *Open Issues.*

### Module 5 — Where the military-action layer enters
The extension this corpus exists to make. Part 73's design-basis threat bounds a defined adversary; a state actor conducting deliberate military action sits above that boundary, and the licensee is not expected to defend against a military. The military-action layer therefore **supplements the design basis rather than replacing it**, and the whole module is about doing that honestly.

Four mechanisms carry it. The **Consequence-Basis Conditionality Check**: declare the credited barriers, test each against the four characteristics of deliberate action, and state the residual — so a relaxation earned in the accident domain is never silently assumed to hold in the adversarial one. **Correlated-barrier cutsets**: a deliberate adversary is a common-cause failure that selects which barrier to take first, which is not what redundancy was designed against. The **dual design basis** and the seam between them, where the Command Broker governs the autonomy boundary. And the two verification questions — what it would take to admit machine-learning-provenance logic onto a safety path, and how deterministic intent relates to a probabilistic risk assessment without being laundered into a frequency it does not have.
Closes with: *Open Issues.*

## Bridge — from framework to filing

### Module 6 — Assembling a submittal
What makes a package reviewable rather than merely complete. The three-track structure — orientation and transmittal, core design basis, supporting instruments — and the reading order a Reviewer's Guide imposes on it. The conformance and traceability matrices as complementary instruments: one maps outward to the regulation, the other inward to the instrument that carries each requirement, and together they make an orphaned requirement visible. Anticipated requests for additional information as a way of compressing the review cycle by answering the hard questions before they are asked.

Classification and docket handling, which is where the public-record tradition meets Safeguards Information, and where the class-versus-derivative resolution split does the real work.

The module closes on the house rule: **name the gap, name the vehicle**. Where the record does not conform, say so and say what would close it. This is the discipline that makes a package usable on a docket, and it is the reason the register exists.

## The Issue Register

Sixteen entries, each one question, each traced to its locus in the corpus, its regulatory hook, and the party who could actually move it. Four diagnoses recur: authority is missing, guidance is absent, the threat basis is not calibrated, or the method has not been developed.

Read [How to Read the Register](register/README.md) first — it explains the seven fields and, more importantly, what the register deliberately does not do.
