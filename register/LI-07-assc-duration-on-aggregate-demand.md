# LI-07 — Coping Duration on Aggregate Campus Demand

Modern reactor designs are built to hold themselves in a safe condition without operator action and without electrical power for a period of time — the coping duration. The industry floor is seventy-two hours. Designs claim more, on the strength of passive cooling: weeks for a shared immersion pool, about a week for per-module isolation condensers, effectively unlimited for an evaporative air-cooled reservoir once replenished. Those durations are certified for a module. Nobody has settled what they are for a campus of modules all demanding the heat sink at once.

**The issue** — What governs the coping duration when every module on a campus actuates simultaneously: the seventy-two-hour class floor, the single-module certified duration, or a family-specific duration computed on aggregate decay heat.

**Where it surfaces** — DBA-MA-SMR-FC1 §5A.6 and its ultimate-heat-sink taxonomy; the thermal-hydraulic treatment is in the multi-module adequacy technical basis, which carries its own citable identifier.

**Regulatory hook** — The coping-duration and mitigating-strategies framework anchoring the seventy-two-hour floor; the General Design Criteria on residual heat removal and on sharing among units.

**Why it is open** — **The method has not been developed** at the level a reviewer can check. The corpus states the governing rule — the aggregate simultaneous-actuation duration, bounded below by the class floor and governed above it by the family-certified duration on aggregate demand, with the single-module figure explicitly *not* the governing value — but the numerical attachment sits at derivative resolution in a calibration register rather than in class-level guidance.

**Closure vehicle and owner** — A family-specific aggregate demonstration accepted on the record, with the heat-sink architecture taxonomy as the organizing frame: shared immersed pool, per-module isolation-condenser pools with their shared-makeup concern, and passive air or evaporative cooling. **Owner: the applicant**, family by family; **the Commission** if the aggregate basis is to be stated in guidance rather than settled case by case.

**State of play** — *As of September 2026.* The class-level rule is closed within the facility-class instrument; the numbers are not public. Note the related and separable point that the artificial-intelligence governance lock endurance is keyed to the seventy-two-hour class floor rather than to the family duration — see [LI-09](LI-09-ml-provenance-safety-logic.md). The aggregation question here is the same one asked of inventory in [LI-06](LI-06-aggregate-inventory-in-the-epz-demonstration.md).

## Reader's stake

*For the practitioner* — This is the adequacy question with the sharpest cliff edge, and the seventy-two-hour figure is where reviewers get misled: it is a floor, not the design duration, and citing it as though it were the answer invites the follow-up that unravels a campus case. The demonstration worth writing sizes the passive window against aggregate decay heat and identifies where the margin ends, rather than asserting a duration.

*For everyone else* — The claim is that these reactors can look after themselves for days without power or operators. For one reactor, that is well established. For a dozen sharing the same cooling water at the same moment, the honest answer is that it depends on the design, and the number for a full campus is not something that has been published.
