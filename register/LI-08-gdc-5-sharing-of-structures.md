# LI-08 — Sharing of Structures, Systems and Components

One of the General Design Criteria says that where nuclear units share equipment important to safety, the sharing must not significantly impair the ability to perform safety functions — including during an accident in one unit while the others keep operating. It was written for a two-unit station sharing a few systems. A small modular campus shares nearly everything: control room, service water, electrical distribution, the heat sink itself. The criterion still applies; what it requires at that degree of sharing has never been worked out.

**The issue** — How the sharing criterion applies to a campus whose modules share most of what matters, and what "does not significantly impair" means when the shared item is the credited heat sink.

**Where it surfaces** — The conformance mapping in candidate work product 2A, group B, identifies this as the strongest regulatory hook for the multi-module configuration. The correlated-defeat treatment is in the defense-in-depth instrument.

**Regulatory hook** — The General Design Criterion on sharing of structures, systems and components in 10 CFR Part 50 Appendix A, read with the criteria on residual heat removal and on electric power systems.

**Why it is open** — **Guidance is absent.** The criterion is clear and the application is not. This is the clearest instance of the pattern the conformance matrix repeatedly records: the design conforms to the *principle* while extending the *application envelope* well beyond what the criterion's drafters had in view.

**Closure vehicle and owner** — Either an applicant demonstration accepted on the record, establishing the referable interpretation, or interpretive guidance from the staff. **Owner: the Commission** for interpretation; **the applicant** for the demonstration. This entry is load-bearing for several others — [LI-06](LI-06-aggregate-inventory-in-the-epz-demonstration.md), [LI-07](LI-07-assc-duration-on-aggregate-demand.md) and [LI-15](LI-15-simultaneous-multi-module-trip.md) are all, in different vocabularies, asking what sharing at campus scale does to a safety demonstration.

**State of play** — *As of September 2026.* Open. Candidate 2A maps it and states the conformance position; the corpus's substantive contribution is the correlated-barrier analysis — shared supports, bays, feeds and paths are exactly what lets a single deliberate act defeat trains that redundancy assumed would fail independently.

## Reader's stake

*For the practitioner* — This criterion is where a campus design is most likely to attract a finding that cannot be closed by adding redundancy, because adding a redundant train that runs through the same shared bay does not reduce the correlated cutset. The demonstration that survives review is a cutset argument, not a count of trains.

*For everyone else* — Sharing equipment between reactors saves money and it means a single problem can affect several reactors at once. There is a rule about this, written when plants shared a little. Nobody has yet decided what it demands when they share almost everything.
