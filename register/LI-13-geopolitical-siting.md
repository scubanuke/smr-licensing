# LI-13 — Geopolitical Siting

Reactor siting rules ask about earthquakes, floods, nearby industrial hazards, and how many people live close by. They do not ask whether a site is near something an adversary would want to strike for reasons having nothing to do with the reactor — a port, a base, a corridor, a border. For a design whose whole value proposition is siting near the load it serves, that omission has consequences.

**The issue** — Siting criteria do not account for proximity to targets of deliberate strategic interest, which is a distinct consideration from the natural and industrial hazards the criteria do cover.

**Where it surfaces** — DBA-MA-SMR-FC1 §9.4, spun out into a standalone geopolitical siting methodology instrument.

**Regulatory hook** — The reactor site criteria of 10 CFR Part 100 and the General Design Criterion on protection against natural phenomena, neither of which reaches strategic proximity. **The absence is the point** — the closest existing analogue is the treatment of nearby hazardous facilities, which is the right structural precedent and the wrong hazard.

**Why it is open** — **The method has not been developed.** There is no accepted way to characterize strategic proximity, and there are good reasons the framework has not tried: the characterization is inherently intelligence-informed, it changes with geopolitics rather than geology, and publishing it would itself be a targeting aid. Those obstacles are real, which is why this entry has been open longest.

**Closure vehicle and owner** — The corpus carries a methodology instrument at class resolution, structured so that the sensitive instantiation lives in derivatives rather than in the public class document — the same mechanism as [LI-12](LI-12-public-docket-and-safeguards-split.md). Regulatory adoption would require the Commission to decide that strategic proximity is a siting consideration at all, which is a policy question rather than a technical one. **Owner: the Commission** for that decision; **the interagency** for anything that requires an intelligence-informed characterization; **the applicant** for applying the methodology to a candidate site.

**State of play** — *As of September 2026.* Open. The methodology instrument exists at working-draft status. This entry compounds with [LI-05](LI-05-reduced-epz-conditionality.md): a reduced planning zone permits siting nearer to population, and strategic proximity is a second reason a site can be close to something it would rather not be close to. Neither consideration is currently weighed against the siting-for-load benefit in any structured way.

## Reader's stake

*For the practitioner* — This will not be closed by a submittal and should not be presented as though it could be. What a submittal can do is show that the applicant considered it, carry the class methodology by reference, and keep the site-specific instantiation in a derivative — which converts an unanswerable question into a documented and reviewable practice.

*For everyone else* — A small reactor is attractive partly because it can be built close to the industry or city that needs the power. Where those places are is not random, and neither is where a conflict would be fought. The rules for choosing a site were written around floods and earthquakes, and this is the argument that they should also, somehow, account for that.
