# LI-11 — The Warning Pathway and Who Decides

If a government agency learned that a nuclear plant was about to be attacked, there is no standing, exercised pathway by which that warning reaches the plant in time to be acted on. Hurricanes have one — a forecast arrives, the plant prepares, everyone has practised. Deliberate threats do not, because the knowledge sits inside classified channels and the plant sits outside them. The agencies with the authority to act may lack the information that says to act; the agencies with the information lack authority over the reactor.

**The issue** — There is no standing, exercised information pathway from defense and intelligence agencies to a licensee, and no settled allocation of who decides that the plant should transition to a protected state.

**Where it surfaces** — Named in the United States regulatory case at Level 1; the trigger framework and monitoring guidance at Levels 3 and 4 carry the receiving end. Candidate work product 3D develops the protocol and carries its own citable identifier.

**Regulatory hook** — **None on the sending end, and that is the issue.** The receiving end is licensing-addressable — the Commission can require a licensee to maintain a capability to receive and act on a warning. The sending end would need authority of the kind Congress has previously created for information sharing in the energy sector on the grid-security axis, extended to the nuclear-safety axis.

**Why it is open** — **Authority is missing**, asymmetrically. One half of the pathway can be built by regulation and the other half cannot.

**Closure vehicle and owner** — A standing, exercised, tearline pathway feeding a licensee-owned decision. **Owner: the interagency** — the Commission, Defense, Homeland Security and Energy jointly — for the pathway; **Congress** for the sending-end authority; **the Commission** for requiring and exercising the receiving capability.

The corpus resolves the decision-authority question on bedrock nuclear-safety grounds, and the resolution is worth stating precisely because it dissolves the objection people reach for first. Entering the protected state is a *protective action*, and an operator must always be able to go safe on its own authority. The facility transitions on its own local trigger observables or on any warning received; no external actor can prevent it. A defense or intelligence warning is therefore an **earlier trigger, not a gate** — its value is bought time, not authority over the reactor. The pathway moves a *conclusion* rather than sources: a graded warning, advisory or directive at high confidence, so agencies can share what to do without disclosing how they know. Local observables flow upward into the fused picture in return. The allocation is deliberately asymmetric — the agencies decide when to issue, the facility decides when to transition, neither can block the other, and the Commission's role is to require and exercise the capability rather than to make the real-time call. It holds because the consequences are lopsided: the protected state is cheap and reversible, so over-triggering is tolerable, and a false alarm costs a stand-down while a missed warning costs the plant.

**State of play** — *As of September 2026.* Open. Candidate 3D advanced to v0.2 in August 2026 to carry the decision-authority treatment above, following review comment. The v0.2 revision also made the receiving capability explicitly **redundant and distributed** — several cleared, pre-authorized decision-makers in separate but nearby locations, each able to initiate the transition, so that a single strike, seizure or communications loss cannot remove the authority. That is the same correlated-common-cause logic applied to barriers in [LI-08](LI-08-gdc-5-sharing-of-structures.md): a decision-maker is a barrier too.

## Reader's stake

*For the practitioner* — The receiving end is the part a submittal can actually address, and addressing it is what converts an interagency lament into a licensing commitment. Specify the capability, its redundancy and distribution, and the exercise obligation; leave the sending-end authority named as an outward-directed finding rather than folded into the licensee's scope.

*For everyone else* — The reassuring part is that nobody outside the plant gets a veto over shutting it down safely; the operators can always act on what they see. The gap is that a warning which could buy them hours has no reliable way to reach them, and building that route requires several federal agencies to agree on something none of them can do alone.
