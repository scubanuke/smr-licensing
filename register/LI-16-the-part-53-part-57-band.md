# LI-16 — The Part 53 / Part 57 Band

There are now two modern licensing frameworks for reactors that are not large light-water plants. Part 53 is final and technology-inclusive. Part 57 is proposed, and drawn around microreactors and designs of comparable risk profile — very small, very low consequence, licensed with proportionately less machinery. The near-term integral small modular reactor class sits above Part 57's band and inside Part 53's. Where exactly the boundary runs, and what an applicant near it should do, has not been stated.

**The issue** — The boundary between the framework bands is not stated in terms an applicant can apply, leaving pathway selection near the boundary a matter of argument rather than of criterion.

**Where it surfaces** — Candidate work product 2A, group A, which supplies the applicability statement and pathway selection for the facility-class instrument.

**Regulatory hook** — 10 CFR Part 53, final; 10 CFR Part 57, proposed. The ADVANCE Act sits behind both.

**Why it is open** — **Guidance is absent**, and partly because one of the two rules is not final. A proposed rule's band cannot be authoritative, so the boundary is unsettled by construction until Part 57 concludes.

**Closure vehicle and owner** — Conclusion of the Part 57 rulemaking with a stated band, and applicability guidance for designs near it. **Owner: the Commission.** In the interim the applicant owns making and defending a placement — the corpus places the near-term integral class, roughly seventy-seven to three hundred megawatts electric, above Part 57's low-consequence band and cites Part 57 for its gating *principle* rather than as its route.

**State of play** — *As of September 2026.* Open. Part 53 was finalized in March 2026 with a correction in April; Part 57 was proposed in May 2026. The dated detail here is deliberately confined to this entry and to Module 1's snapshot unit — see [Snapshot: 2026 Rule Status](../core/module-1-licensing-pathways/90-snapshot-2026-rule-status.md) — so that the rest of the site does not age with the rulemaking.

## Reader's stake

*For the practitioner* — Pathway selection is the first thing a reviewer reads and the last thing anyone wants to revisit. State the placement, state the criterion it rests on, and cite the adjacent rule for the principle rather than implying eligibility under it. A placement argued once, explicitly, is far cheaper than a placement assumed and later questioned.

*For everyone else* — There are now two sets of rules for new reactor designs, one for very small ones and one for everything else that is not a traditional large plant. The reactors most likely to be built next fall near the line between them, and the line has not been drawn precisely yet — which is one reason these projects take as long as they do.
