# LI-05 — Reduced-EPZ Conditionality and Proximity Coupling

The emergency planning zone around a reactor is sized by calculation, not by tradition, and for a small modular design the calculation can support a zone far smaller than the ten-mile ring most people picture — in leading near-term designs, something between the site boundary and roughly two miles. That reduction is real and it is earned. It is also calculated for *accidents*. A deliberate adversary can do things an accident cannot, including defeating the specific barriers the small number depends on. And because a smaller zone is what permits siting nearer to population, the favorable accident arithmetic slightly raises the consequence of its own residual.

**The issue** — A consequence relaxation earned in the accident domain does not automatically bound the adversarial domain, and the corpus has no accepted regulatory mechanism for carrying the resulting residual forward.

**Where it surfaces** — DBA-MA-SMR-FC1 §3A.4 and §3A.6, resting on the Conditionality Check introduced in DBA-MA-ST. Candidate work product 3A lifts the whole argument into a standalone technical basis.

**Regulatory hook** — 10 CFR 50.47 and Appendix E to Part 50, as modified by the 2023 emergency-preparedness rule for small modular reactors and other new technologies; Regulatory Guide 1.183 for the source term; the Environmental Protection Agency's one-rem early-phase protective-action guideline as the dose threshold.

**Why it is open** — **The method has not been developed.** Nothing in the emergency-preparedness framework is wrong; it simply has no place to record that a consequence relaxation is conditional on barriers that a deliberate act can defeat. Absent such a place, the conditionality either goes unstated or gets buried where the affected public cannot see it.

**Closure vehicle and owner** — The corpus proposes the Consequence-Basis Conditionality Check — declare the credited barriers, test each against the four characteristics of deliberate action, state the residual — registered as a mandatory cross-sector requirement and instantiated at facility-class level. Making it *binding and reviewable* would take Commission action. **Owner: the Commission** for the regulatory status; **the applicant** for performing and disclosing the check in any event. The corpus position is explicitly that the residual is carried as a design-basis-military-action input with scalable protective action, **not** by re-enlarging the accident planning zone.

**State of play** — *As of September 2026.* The Check is registered at series level and worked at facility-class level; candidate 3A is drafted and carries its own citable identifier. It has no regulatory standing. The proximity-coupling point — that a contracted zone places a nearer population against the adversarial residual — is stated openly in the corpus rather than elided, and that disclosure choice is itself part of the open question.

## Reader's stake

*For the practitioner* — This is the claim a reviewer scrutinizes hardest, and the temptation is to defend the reduced zone by arguing that the adversarial case is bounded by it. It is not, and saying so is the stronger position: the relaxation survives on its own accident-domain terms, the residual is declared separately, and the protective-action concept is keyed to it. An applicant who blurs the two has made the reduced zone look like a trick.

*For everyone else* — The smaller emergency zone is not a waiver and not a favor; the company has to prove it. What it is calculated for is an accident. A deliberate attack is a different problem, and you are entitled to ask exactly what the calculation assumes and what it admits it does not cover — and to ask whether that answer is on the public record or hidden in a protected annex.
