# LI-14 — Under-Frequency Load Shedding Obligations

The electrical grid runs at a fixed frequency, and when demand outruns generation the frequency sags. The grid's automatic defence is to shed load in stages. Generators are expected to stay connected and ride through that sag rather than tripping off and making it worse. What a fleet of small modules on one campus is obliged to do during such an event — ride through together, trip together, or something in between — has not been settled, and the answer matters more for twelve small units at one point on the grid than for one large one.

**The issue** — The under-frequency ride-through and load-shedding obligations of a multi-module campus have not been stated, and the campus's aggregate behaviour during a grid frequency excursion is undefined.

**Where it surfaces** — DBA-MA-SMR-FC1 §9.7, carried as an open item with a position paper anticipated and not yet written.

**Regulatory hook** — The General Design Criterion on electric power systems, read against the reliability standards governing under-frequency load shedding and generator ride-through, which are the grid regulator's instruments rather than the nuclear regulator's.

**Why it is open** — **Guidance is absent**, and it is absent across a jurisdictional seam. The grid reliability standards address generators; the nuclear framework addresses the plant's own safety response; a campus of modules sits where the two meet, and neither body has written for it.

**Closure vehicle and owner** — A position paper establishing the campus behaviour, taken into whichever forum owns the standard. **Owner: the applicant and the grid operator** jointly for the behaviour; **the reliability organization** for any standards change; **the Commission** only for the safety-side consequences of whatever behaviour is adopted. Of everything on this register, this is the entry least dependent on the military-action layer and most likely to be resolved by ordinary engineering practice.

**State of play** — *As of September 2026.* Open and not yet worked. Named in the facility-class instrument's open-items list; the anticipated position paper has not been drafted. It is the least developed entry here and is listed so that it is not quietly lost.

## Reader's stake

*For the practitioner* — This is a small item that becomes a large one late, because grid interconnection studies and the safety analysis have to agree, and they are usually produced by different teams on different schedules. Settling the campus ride-through posture early is cheap; discovering a conflict during interconnection review is not.

*For everyone else* — When the grid gets into trouble, power stations are supposed to hold on rather than dropping off and making things worse. Nobody has yet written down what a site with a dozen small reactors should do in that moment — which matters because a dozen units dropping off together is a much bigger event than one.
