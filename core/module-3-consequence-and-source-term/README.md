# Module 3 — Consequence and the Source Term

## Learning Objectives

After this module, a reader will be able to:

- Trace the chain from source term through dose to emergency planning zone, and say what governs each link.
- Explain what a mechanistic source term is and why certified quantities are carried by design-family reference rather than reproduced.
- State the basis on which a planning zone may be smaller than the familiar ten-mile ring, and what the applicant must prove to earn it.
- Show why a per-module source term does not carry to a multi-module campus.

## What this module does

This is the module the rest of the consequence argument hangs from. A planning zone is not a danger line drawn by custom; it is the outcome of a calculation. How much radioactive material could be released, what dose that produces at what distance under what weather, and where that dose falls below the threshold at which a protective action is worth taking.

Smaller reactors with more retentive barriers produce a smaller number at the top of that chain, so the zone at the bottom can be smaller too. That is legitimate, and it is also the single most scrutinized claim in a small modular reactor application — because it is the claim that lets a plant be sited near the load it serves.
