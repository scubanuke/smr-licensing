# From Dose to Zone

The ring on the map around a nuclear plant is not a danger line and never was. It is a **planning** boundary: the area within which protective actions — shelter, evacuation, distribution of potassium iodide — are worked out in advance, exercised, and ready to be ordered. Outside it, the judgment is that protective action is not warranted, not that nothing could ever reach there.

The familiar ten-mile figure attached itself to the large light-water fleet and, through long familiarity, came to feel like a property of nuclear plants rather than an outcome of a calculation done for a particular class of them. It is the second thing. And once it is understood as a calculation, the possibility of a different answer for a different design stops being suspicious and becomes a question about inputs.

## The chain

Four links, each with its own governing consideration.

**Source term.** What escapes, from the previous unit.

**Atmospheric transport and dispersion.** How that release travels and dilutes. Wind speed and direction, atmospheric stability, precipitation, terrain, the duration and height of the release. This is where most of the variability lives.

**Dose at distance.** The projected dose a person would receive at a given distance, over a given exposure period, through the relevant pathways — plume immersion, inhalation, ground deposition. The early phase is what sizes the zone.

**Comparison against a threshold.** For a design proceeding under the 2023 route, the criterion is stated in the regulation itself: whether public dose from radioactive releases is **projected to exceed 1 rem total effective dose equivalent over 96 hours**. Three features of that phrasing matter — it is expressed in dose rather than distance, it is *projected* and so concerns a postulated release rather than a measurement, and it integrates over a stated 96-hour window that bounds what the analysis must cover.

The figure aligns with the Environmental Protection Agency's early-phase protective action guide, which is the underlying judgment about when a protective action is worth its own disruption. But the binding criterion an applicant is analyzed against is the one in the rule, and citing the guide in its place is a small imprecision that a reviewer will notice.

The zone boundary falls where the projected dose drops below that threshold. Smaller source term, shorter distance. That is the whole of it.

## What the 2023 rule changed

The emergency-preparedness rule for small modular reactors and other new technologies, final in November 2023, made the zone **performance-based and consequence-scaled** rather than fixed by convention.

The significance is procedural as much as technical. Before it, an applicant seeking a smaller zone was asking for an exemption from an established figure, with everything that implies about burden and posture. After it, the applicant is doing what the rule asks: demonstrating the consequence, and receiving the zone that consequence supports. The reduction is *earned on the record* rather than *granted as relief*, and for near-term designs the result runs from roughly the site boundary out to about two miles.

That distinction matters for how this is talked about in public. A community told that a company received a waiver has been told something misleading. A community told that the company had to prove a smaller number and did has been told the truth — and is then in a position to ask what the proof assumed, which is the right question.

## Across a meteorological envelope

Four words carry more weight than anything else in this unit, and a reviewer's attention goes to them first.

A dose calculation needs weather. Choose favorable conditions — a brisk steady wind, a well-mixed atmosphere, dispersion working hard — and the dose at any distance is low. Choose a stable atmosphere with a light persistent wind holding the plume together along one sector, and the same release produces a very different number.

A single favorable case is not a demonstration. What the analysis must do is run across an **envelope** of meteorological conditions representative of the site, and show the criterion met at an appropriately conservative point within it rather than at a convenient one. The questions a reviewer asks are consequently specific: which meteorological data set, over what period of record, what percentile is the result reported at, how were stability classes and wind persistence treated, and was the release duration matched to the scenario rather than assumed short.

The general lesson generalizes past this module. When a favorable result depends on a distribution, the honest presentation states where in the distribution the result sits. Naming the percentile is the difference between a demonstration and an anecdote.

## What the zone buys

There is a reason applicants care about this beyond compliance, and stating it plainly is better than leaving it to be inferred.

A smaller planning zone makes a site viable that a ten-mile zone would not. Emergency planning is a genuine constraint on siting — it requires population within the zone to be plannable-for, which in practice keeps large plants away from dense areas. Contract the zone and a plant can sit near the industrial load, the data centre campus, or the city it serves, which removes transmission cost and loss and is much of the economic case for the technology.

That is legitimate. It is also the beginning of the sharpest honest point in this corpus, because siting nearer to population means the population is nearer to whatever residual remains. The accident arithmetic and the adversarial residual pull in opposite directions, and the full treatment of that is the Conditionality Check in [Module 5](../module-5-military-action-layer/01-the-conditionality-check.md), with the open form at [LI-05](../../register/LI-05-reduced-epz-conditionality.md).

Nothing in that undermines this unit's conclusion. The zone is properly earned for what it was calculated against. The question is what it was calculated against.

## What a reviewer should press on

Whether the meteorological envelope is stated and the result located within it, or whether a single case is presented as though it were bounding.

Whether the exposure pathways and the early-phase duration match the release the source term actually describes.

And whether the demonstration used one module's inventory or the campus total — which is the next unit, and the most consequential input of all.

## What to carry forward

The zone is a planning boundary produced by a calculation, not a danger line fixed by convention. The chain runs source term, dispersion, dose, comparison against the one-rem early-phase guideline. Since 2023 a smaller zone is earned by demonstration rather than granted by exemption. The phrase that carries the weight is *across a meteorological envelope*, and an honest result names where in the distribution it sits. And the reason any of this is worth doing commercially — siting near load — is the same reason the residual deserves saying out loud.
