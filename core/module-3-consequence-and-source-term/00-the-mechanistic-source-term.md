# The Mechanistic Source Term

Everything in this module descends from one number, and it is worth being clear about what the number is before anything is done with it. The source term is how much radioactive material could escape to the environment in a given scenario — how much, of which isotopes, in what chemical and physical form, released over what period.

It is not a measure of how much radioactive material the plant contains. A reactor core holds an enormous inventory and almost all of it stays where it is under almost every postulated condition. The source term is the fraction that gets past every barrier between the fuel and the outside, which is why it is a statement about the *design* rather than about the fuel.

## Prescribed fractions, and the move away from them

For most of the history of light-water reactor licensing, the source term was prescribed. A regulatory position stated what fraction of each radionuclide group should be assumed released — a gap-activity release and an early in-vessel release, with different numbers for boiling-water and pressurized-water designs — and every applicant used those fractions whether or not their design warranted them.

That approach has an obvious virtue: it is uniform, conservative, and impossible to game. It also has an obvious cost: a design whose barriers are genuinely more retentive gets no credit for it, because the analysis was told what to assume before it looked.

The alternative source term framework changed that. It permits a release characterization grounded in the physics and chemistry of the specific design — timing, magnitude, isotopic composition, chemical form — rather than a prescribed table. That is what "mechanistic" means here: the release follows from a mechanism that can be examined, rather than from a convention.

The class-level instrument in this corpus carries both. It reproduces the prescriptive baseline by radionuclide group, because that is the yardstick a reviewer has in mind, and alongside it a normalized barrier set by design family. Having them adjacent is the point — it lets a reader see exactly what the newer designs are claiming credit for.

## The barriers, and what each one actually does

Four barriers stand between the fuel and the environment, and the interesting differences between designs are in the last two.

The **fuel matrix** itself retains most fission products within the ceramic. The **cladding** contains what escapes the matrix, and the gap between them holds the small inventory that a gap release refers to. The **primary system boundary** contains what escapes the cladding. And **containment** is the final structure.

For the near-term small modular designs, the retention mechanism between primary system and environment is where the families diverge, and this is where the reader's choice from Module 0 starts paying.

A **pool-immersed integral pressurized-water** design puts the entire primary system inside a compact containment immersed in a large pool of water. Material escaping the primary system passes through water, and water is an extremely effective scrubber for the soluble and particulate species that dominate dose. The credit claimed is aqueous scrubbing.

A **dry containment with an evaporative reservoir** takes a different route: the containment is not flooded, and the retention argument rests on the containment's own integrity and on passive heat removal keeping conditions inside it within bounds.

A **steel-plate-composite containment on an isolation-condenser basis** claims something more fundamental — that the design's response to the relevant events does not uncover the core at all, so the later stages of the release progression are not reached.

Those are three genuinely different claims, and a residual that belongs to one does not belong to the others. A drained pool is a pool-immersed design's problem and is meaningless for an air-cooled one.

## Carried by reference, and why that is legitimate

An applicant does not reproduce the certified radionuclide inventories, decontamination factors, and mechanistic release fractions for their design family. Those are design-certification quantities, established once in the certification record and carried into later analyses by reference.

Newcomers often read this as evasion — the load-bearing numbers are somewhere else, so how is the argument checkable? Two reasons it is not.

First, the quantities are **established once and reviewed once**, which is the entire premise of design certification and of the topical-report economy in Module 1. Re-deriving them in every application would not make them more correct; it would make them inconsistent, and it would mean the same numbers were reviewed repeatedly by different staff to different conclusions.

Second, and more important for this site, **carrying them by reference is what keeps a class-level instrument site-neutral.** A document that reproduced design-specific certified quantities alongside site-specific analysis would be a derivative in everything but name, and would run straight into the classification problem of [LI-12](../../register/LI-12-public-docket-and-safeguards-split.md). The reference is a structural choice as much as an economical one.

What an applicant *must* do is state which family's barrier set they are crediting and which certified values they are carrying, precisely enough that a reviewer can go and check them. A reference without that specificity is the evasion the newcomer suspected.

## What a reviewer should press on

Which barrier set is credited, stated explicitly rather than left implied by the design description — because everything in the next two units resolves differently depending on the answer.

Whether the retention mechanism credited is available in all the conditions the analysis covers, or only in the ones where it works best. Aqueous scrubbing credits water being where the analysis assumed it would be.

And whether the referenced certified quantities are identified to a version, so the reference resolves to something specific rather than to a design family in general.

## What to carry forward

The source term is what escapes, not what is present, and it is a claim about the design rather than about the fuel. The move from prescribed fractions to a mechanistic characterization is what lets a more retentive design claim credit for being one. The retention mechanism differs by family — aqueous scrubbing, dry containment with passive heat removal, or a no-core-uncovery basis — and a residual belongs to a barrier set rather than to reactors in general. Certified quantities are carried by reference, which is both economical and what keeps a class-level instrument site-neutral.
