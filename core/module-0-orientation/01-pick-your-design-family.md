# Pick Your Design Family

This site asks you to make one choice, here, and carry it through everything that follows.

Pick a design family. Not a vendor and not a product — an architecture, characterized by how it keeps radioactive material inside and how it removes heat when nothing is helping it. Three archetypes cover the near-term light-water field well enough for the purpose.

## The three

**Pool-immersed integral pressurized-water.** The whole primary system — core, steam generator, pressurizer — sits inside one vessel, and that vessel sits inside a compact containment immersed in a large pool of water. Material escaping the primary system passes through water, and water scrubs the soluble and particulate species that dominate dose. Heat goes into the pool, which boils off slowly; on an aggregate campus demand the coping window is measured in weeks. The credited retention mechanism is **aqueous scrubbing**, and the shared pool is shared across modules by construction.

**Isolation-condenser boiling-water.** Each module has its own condenser pools, and decay heat is removed by natural circulation to them without alternating-current power. Containment is dry. The coping window is measured in days on a subset of trains. The credited basis leans on **not uncovering the core** in the relevant events, so the later stages of a release progression are not reached. The modules are genuinely independent — until the pools need makeup, which typically comes from a common source.

**Dry passive-air or evaporative.** Containment is dry and heat is rejected to the atmosphere through passive air flow or an evaporative reservoir. Once the reservoir is replenished the coping duration is effectively unlimited. The credited retention rests on **containment integrity and passive heat removal** rather than on a water path.

Pick one. If you have no reason to prefer any, take the pool-immersed case: it is the one whose credited barriers are most obviously shared across modules, which makes the campus arguments easiest to see.

## Why this is not decorative

It would be easy to treat this as a framing device — pick something so the text has an addressee. It is not, and the reason is a rule that recurs throughout the site:

**A residual belongs to a barrier set, not to a reactor type.**

When the Conditionality Check in Module 5 asks what remains after the credited barriers are tested against deliberate action, the answer depends entirely on which barriers were credited. A drained pool is a residual that belongs to the pool-immersed family and is meaningless for an air-cooled one. A shared-makeup vulnerability belongs to the per-module condenser architecture and does not arise where the heat sink is a single shared body of water. Conversely, the shared pool that gives the first family its long coping window is a single correlated barrier across every module on the site, which the second family does not have in the same form.

Three places where this bites:

The **source term** in Module 3, where the retention mechanism you credit is family-specific and so is the way it can fail.

The **Conditionality Check** in Module 5, where the residual you declare is the residual your barrier set leaves, and a Check performed generically produces generic conclusions, which is to say none.

The **coping duration** at [LI-07](../../register/LI-07-assc-duration-on-aggregate-demand.md), where weeks, days and unlimited are three genuinely different answers to the same question and the register entry cannot resolve for all three at once.

## How to carry it

One habit, which costs nothing: when you reach a claim about barriers, coping or consequence, stop and ask *is this true of my family?*

Sometimes the answer is yes and the claim is general. Often it is not, and the text will say so — the units are written to flag where a statement is family-specific. Occasionally you will find a claim that reads as general and is not, and noticing that is worth more than anything this site can teach you directly, because that is precisely the error the corpus exists to prevent at a larger scale.

## A note on what these archetypes are and are not

They are **archetypes**, drawn to make the analytical differences visible. Real designs vary, and no vendor's actual product is exactly any of these. The site deliberately does not name products, for two reasons: the argument is about architecture rather than about anyone's engineering, and a document that characterized specific vendors' safety cases from outside would be doing something it is not in a position to do well.

If you are working on a real design, map it onto the nearest archetype and note where it differs. The differences are usually where the interesting questions are.

## What to carry forward

Three archetypes: pool-immersed with aqueous scrubbing and a shared pool measured in weeks; per-module isolation condensers with a no-core-uncovery basis measured in days and a shared-makeup concern; dry passive-air or evaporative, effectively unlimited once replenished. Pick one and hold it. A residual belongs to a barrier set rather than to a reactor type, which is why the choice is load-bearing in Modules 3 and 5 and at LI-07. And the habit that makes it work is asking, at every claim, whether it is true of your family.
