# Module 0 — Orientation

## Learning Objectives

After this module, a reader will be able to:

- Describe what a licensing action is, who takes part in it, and what each party can and cannot decide.
- Distinguish the safety case from the security case and say why they are reviewed under separate parts of Title 10.
- Explain why a small modular reactor poses licensing questions that are not simply scaled-down large-reactor questions.
- Choose a design family and state the credited barrier set that choice commits them to.

## What this module does

Reactor licensing is an adversarial-in-form but cooperative-in-practice exchange between an applicant who wants permission to build and operate, and a federal regulator who grants it only on a written finding of reasonable assurance of adequate protection. Everything else — the volume of paper, the years of schedule, the review machinery in Module 2 — follows from the fact that the finding has to be written down and defended publicly.

This module establishes the vocabulary and the cast, and then asks the reader to make one choice that will matter for the rest of the site.

## Units

**[What a Licensing Action Is](00-what-a-licensing-action-is.md)** — the application, the docket, the staff review, the finding. The parties: applicant, NRC staff, the Advisory Committee on Reactor Safeguards, the Commission, the Nuclear Energy Institute acting for industry, and intervenors with hearing rights. What each of them can actually move, which turns out to be the most useful thing to know when you reach the register.

**[Pick Your Design Family](01-pick-your-design-family.md)** — three near-term architectures and the barrier sets they credit: a pool-immersed integral pressurized-water design with aqueous scrubbing and aggregate pool boil-off measured in weeks; a boiling-water design with per-module isolation-condenser pools and a coping duration measured in days; a dry passive-air design with an evaporative reservoir that is effectively unlimited once replenished. The reader picks one and carries it forward.

The choice is not decorative. The Conditionality Check in Module 5 resolves differently for each family, because a residual that belongs to a drained pool is not a residual that belongs to an air-cooled reservoir. A reader who skips this choice will find the consequence modules harder than they need to be.
